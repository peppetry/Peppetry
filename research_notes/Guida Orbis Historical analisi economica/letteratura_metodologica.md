## Key Question 1 — Which papers explicitly use or document "Orbis Historical", and what do they say about its coverage, structure and pitfalls?

### Takeaway
"Orbis Historical" (also "Historical Orbis", "Orbis Historic", "Orbis Historical Disk/Data Files") is BvD/Moody's archive product that links successive Orbis vintages by BvD ID into a long firm-year panel; the main documents that describe how to process it are the Kalemli-Özcan team's "Processing Orbis Historical Disk" guide and the 2015→2024 "How to Construct Nationally Representative Firm-Level Data" paper, plus De Loecker–Obermeier–Van Reenen (2022) for the UK component. Documented pitfalls are: multiple records per BvD ID–year (consolidation codes, filing types, changed closing month), heavy missing employment, survivorship in single vintages, and sheer volume (hundreds of GB of flat text files).

### Cited Findings
- Orbis Historical "links several vintages/disks of the Orbis data through firm identifiers to obtain firm-level longitudinal data sets for many countries"; "although the Historical Product avoids many of the issues involved by combining numerous vintages/disks, it requires a certain methodology to process the historical data. Detailed guides and programs ... are available at http://econweb.umd.edu/~kalemli/orbis.html" — [Kalemli-Özcan, Sørensen, Villegas-Sanchez, Volosovych, Yeşiltaş, NBER WP 21558 (rev.)](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w21558/revisions/w21558.rev0.pdf); [2023 AEJ:Macro submission version](https://www.uh.edu/~bsorense/Data_Paper_AEJ_Macro_Submission2023.pdf)
- A dedicated technical guide, "Processing Orbis Historical Disk", reports that the historical disk is around 280 GB, that an 8 TB drive is recommended to process and store the data, and that processing is done with Stata MP version 12 or higher — [Processing Orbis Historical Disk (Kalemli-Özcan site)](https://sebnemkalemliozcan.com/assets/workingpapers/Processing%20Orbis%20Historical%20Disk%20FINAL2.pdf); mirror: [fanjt.weebly.com/orbisdocumentation.pdf](https://fanjt.weebly.com/uploads/1/9/4/7/19473457/orbisdocumentation.pdf)
- File structure as described in the Kalemli-Özcan processing documentation: raw historical files are distributed via FTP "structured in different modules and years", in .rar/.txt form; the processed Stata files include a BvD ID and Name file (`bvdname.dta`), a Legal info file (`legal.dta`) and Industry-Global-Financials-and-Ratios files (`hindgfr.dta`, `hindgfrusd.dta` for the USD version); the financial module "contains both consolidated and unconsolidated balance sheets, income statements, and employment data, presented in a long format where a row corresponds to the data for one entity for one year"; all files carry the BVDID in the first column and are linked on it; a `Firm_description` folder holds name, address, legal form, industry classification and national identifiers organised by 2-letter ISO country code, and a `Financials` folder holds processed firm-level financials — [Processing Orbis Historical Disk](https://sebnemkalemliozcan.com/assets/workingpapers/Processing%20Orbis%20Historical%20Disk%20FINAL2.pdf); [Data_Description_2015_09_final.pdf (UMD)](https://www.econ.umd.edu/sites/www.econ.umd.edu/files/pubs/Data_Description_2015_09_final.pdf)
- In Orbis Historical "companies may report consolidated, unconsolidated, or both types of statements ('accounts'), identified by a 'consolidation code'": U1 = only unconsolidated available, C1 = only consolidated available, U2/C2 = both available; "all firms reporting under different consolidation codes share the same BvD ID number and differ on their account information" — [NBER WP 21558 rev.1](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w21558/revisions/w21558.rev1.pdf)
- Vendor/library descriptions: "ORBIS Historical Data is a point-in-time snapshot of the ORBIS database" — [Yale Library guide "ORBIS Historic"](https://guides.library.yale.edu/bvdorbis/historic). Orbis Historical holds "financials older than the past five to ten years as well as historical ownership data from 2007 onwards"; "Financials are available from 1995 to June 2025, and ownership data is available from 2007 to June 2025"; data come "in structured sets of tab-delimited TXT files, and are updated twice yearly in June and December" — [CBS Library, Orbis Historical Data](https://libguides.cbs.dk/c.php?g=727940&p=5301256). Moody's DataHub "is the brand new home of ORBIS historical company data", a cloud portal allowing export via SFTP/AWS S3 in CSV, Avro, Parquet and ORC, with categories Company Financials & Key Figures, Company Information, Company Reports, Finance, Management & Organisation, M&A, Ownership & Corporate Governance, Patents — [CBS Library newsletter on Moody's DataHub](https://libguides.cbs.dk/blogs/newsletter/5426/smooth-access-to-historical-company-data-with-moody-s-datahub). - De Loecker, Obermeier & Van Reenen (2022), "Firms and inequality" (IFS Deaton Review; CEP DP1838; POID WP 027): main micro data set is "Historical Orbis (HO) ... a relatively new data set from Bureau Van Dijk that provides digitised versions of the available accounts of essentially all incorporated firms in the world", restricted to UK-incorporated firms listed in Companies House. Pitfall: "Historical Orbis contains multiple observations per year for some firms", one reason being multiple 'filing types' — some firms have both an 'Annual Report' and a 'Local Registry Filing' observation. Their Samples A and B "restrict the sample to firms in the 'market economy', remove duplicates, use observations at the highest level of aggregation, and drop firms that have missing values on employment and/or have fewer than 10 employees. Sample A also conditions on non-missing wage bill and EBITDA (but allows missing sales and COGS), while Sample B conditions on non-missing sales and COGS (but allows missing wage bill and EBITDA)" — [IFS online appendices](https://ifs.org.uk/inequality/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Firms-online-appendices-IFS-Deaton-Review-of-Inequality.pdf); [CEP DP1838](https://cep.lse.ac.uk/pubs/download/dp1838.pdf); [POID WP 027](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/119782/1/POID_working_paper_027.pdf); published as [Oxford Open Economics 3 (Suppl. 1), 2024](https://academic.oup.com/ooec/article/3/Supplement_1/i962/7708115)

- Survivorship/vintage pitfall: "Orbis drops non-reporting firms from the database after a certain period of time, and single vintage data will often over-represent larger firms and under-represent smaller firms due to survivorship bias"; "Amadeus provides at most the ten most recent reporting years for the same company while Orbis de facto reports data for the five most recent reporting years at most. - The raw Orbis data "exhibits minimal coverage of small firms, extensive missing data, and poor national representation" and "may contain irregularities which, if not dealt with, will result in data loss during a standard cleaning procedure" — [NBER WP 21558 rev.1](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w21558/revisions/w21558.rev1.pdf)
- Critical assessment: Arndt (2023) "When Should We Believe Research Using ORBIS Firm Data?" notes Orbis claims >400 million firms in 2022, and argues that "the complexity of the data, existing errors and inconsistencies, missing relevant information, the interrelation of these problems, and insufficient documentation and support ... only allow for confidence in the validity of results under very specific conditions", which "will in most cases only apply to a much smaller sample"; Orbis "should only be used for research if sufficient time, resources, and skills are available to fully understand the data and overcome its problems" — [SSRN 4584106](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4584106); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/374170245_When_Should_We_Believe_Research_Using_ORBIS_Firm_Data)
- NBER hosts an "ORBIS Project" / "ORBIS Company Data" page and a presentation "Firm Level Data from ORBIS/AMADEUS Database" by Kalemli-Özcan, plus "NBER-ORBIS Firm Level Data Initiative" slides (Gourinchas & Kalemli-Özcan) on benefits and pitfalls of Orbis — [NBER ORBIS project](https://www.nber.org/research/orbis); [NBER ORBIS Company Data](https://www.nber.org/research/data/orbis-company-data); [Kalemli-Özcan slides](https://data.nber.org/international-finance//Sebnem.pdf); [NBER conference slides f129854](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f129854.slides.pdf)
- Other documents that process Orbis (not necessarily Historical) at scale and document structure: World Bank PRWP 10261 (Businesses of the State) stresses that Orbis "requires extensive 'cleaning' to verify the precise location of subsidiaries on a corporate chain" — [World Bank PRWP 10261](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099800112132221252/pdf/IDU03d9586040b28504839081120922e33694f65.pdf); JRC FOWN manual (Gregori & Nardo, JRC118266) builds an EU foreign-ownership dataset from Orbis ownership files — [JRC118266](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC118266/jrc118266_fown_manual.pdf)

### Inferences
- The only widely circulated, step-by-step technical documentation of the Orbis Historical *flat files* (table names, linking keys, Stata pipeline) is the Kalemli-Özcan team's "Processing Orbis Historical Disk" + UMD orbis.html program suite; De Loecker et al. (2022) is the best-documented *non-Kalemli-Özcan* application and shows that duplicates in Historical Orbis arise not only from consolidation codes but from "filing type" (Annual Report vs Local Registry Filing), which is a distinct deduplication key.
- Snippets conflict on whether Orbis (vs Amadeus) retains exited firms; the safest reading is that Orbis Historical, being an archive of vintages, retains firms that disappeared from later live vintages, whereas any single live download is survivorship-biased.
- The Yale snippet "point in time snapshots of ORBIS back to 2020" is probably a garbled rendering (financials reportedly go back to 1995; ownership to 2007); treat the "2020" date as unverified.

### Gaps
- Could not read the "Processing Orbis Historical Disk" guide itself, so its exact table list (e.g., Key Financials, Industry-Global financials & ratios, Legal info, Contact info, Industry classifications, Ownership links), variable names (CLOSDATE, CONSCODE, NR_MONTHS, FILING_TYPE, ACCPRACTICE, EXCHRATE, UNIT), and its de-duplication order of precedence are unverified.
- No ECB/IMF paper was found that *evaluates* Orbis Historical coverage by year versus live vintages; whether early years (1995–2004) in Orbis Historical are back-filled from later vintages (and therefore still survivorship-biased) is unanswered.

## Key Question 2 — What cleaning steps do the main methodological references prescribe (consolidation codes, duplicates, fiscal year, months, currency, deflators, negatives, outliers, missing values, entry/exit, TFP variables)?

### Takeaway
Across sources the canonical pipeline is: (1) choose account type (most keep unconsolidated U1/U2 plus LF for national representativeness; Díez et al. add a "continuity" rule), (2) de-duplicate BvD ID–year using consolidation code, closing date and filing type, (3) assign fiscal year from the closing date (Kalemli-Özcan: closing date on/after 1 June → current calendar year), (4) drop whole firms with negative total assets/employment/sales/tangible fixed assets or implausible employment (>2 million), (5) drop firm-years with missing/zero/negative output, wage bill, capital or materials, (6) deflate with 2-digit industry deflators and (for TFP) build capital by PIM, and (7) winsorise/trim ratios or growth rates at 1/99 (CompNet, markup literature) or more lightly at 99.9.

### Cited Findings

**Kalemli-Özcan, Sørensen, Villegas-Sanchez, Volosovych & Yeşiltaş (2015 NBER WP 21558; 2024 AEJ: Macroeconomics 16(2):1-22) — brief summary (user already has the appendix)**
- Full citation: "How to Construct Nationally Representative Firm-Level Data from the Orbis Global Database: New Facts on SMEs and Aggregate Implications for Industry Concentration", AEJ: Macroeconomics, vol. 16, no. 2, April 2024 — [AEA page](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Fmac.20220036); [author PDF](https://sebnemkalemliozcan.com/assets/publications/AEJMacro-2022-0036.pdf); earlier versions: [NBER WP 21558 (2015)](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w21558/w21558.pdf), [CEPR DP10829](https://cepr.org/publications/dp10829), [Tinbergen 15-110](https://papers.tinbergen.nl/15110.pdf), [EconStor](https://www.econstor.eu/handle/10419/125116), [UMD Data Description Sept 2015](https://www.econ.umd.edu/sites/www.econ.umd.edu/files/pubs/Data_Description_2015_09_final.pdf)
- Duplicates: "there are a small number of duplicates in terms of BVDID-year", with three reasons: (i) "some firms report both consolidated and unconsolidated accounting reports"; (ii) "some firms change reporting month in a given year, with reports given for both months during the year of the change"; (iii) "a very small number of firms file their reports through two different channels". One way to handle (ii) is "to retain the data for the closing date closest conceptually to the end of year" — [Data Description 2015](https://www.econ.umd.edu/sites/www.econ.umd.edu/files/pubs/Data_Description_2015_09_final.pdf)
- Account types: Orbis also contains accounts of type "LF with limited financial information (number of employees, turnover, shareholders' funds, capital, or some of these items), and NF with no financial items at all" — [Data Description 2015](https://www.econ.umd.edu/sites/www.econ.umd.edu/files/pubs/Data_Description_2015_09_final.pdf)
- Fiscal year: they use the variable "Account Closing Date" (CLOSDATE) to assign the calendar year; "if a closing date is after or on June 1st, the current year is assigned" (complementary rule: closing dates before 1 June → previous calendar year, per the snippet's framing) — [Data Description 2015](https://www.econ.umd.edu/sites/www.econ.umd.edu/files/pubs/Data_Description_2015_09_final.pdf); [NBER WP 21558](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w21558/w21558.pdf)
- Firm-level drops used in this group's work: "drop the entire company (all years) if total assets is negative in any year"; drop companies "if employment (in persons) is negative in any year and companies with employment larger than that of Walmart (2 million) in any year" — summarised as Kalemli-Özcan et al. (2019) cleaning in [Gourinchas, Kalemli-Özcan, Penciakova & Sander, NBER WP 27877 rev.0](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w27877/revisions/w27877.rev0.pdf) (published JEEA; SSRN [3700704](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3700704))
- A snippet in the same result set stated that duplicates are "eliminated based on BvD ID keeping the consolidated accounts when both consolidated and unconsolidated are reported, to avoid double counting of sales" — [NBER WP 21558 rev.1](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w21558/revisions/w21558.rev1.pdf). **Gal, P. (2013), "Measuring Total Factor Productivity at the Firm Level using OECD-ORBIS", OECD Economics Department Working Papers No. 1049, ECO/WKP(2013)41, 21 May 2013, DOI 10.1787/5k46dsb25ls6-en**
- Links: [OECD PDF](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2013/05/measuring-total-factor-productivity-at-the-firm-level-using-oecd-orbis_g17a22d0/5k46dsb25ls6-en.pdf); [OECD iLibrary page](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/measuring-total-factor-productivity-at-the-firm-level-using-oecd-orbis_5k46dsb25ls6-en.html); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/oec/ecoaaa/1049-en.html); [ECO/WKP(2013)41](https://one.oecd.org/document/ECO/WKP(2013)41/En/pdf)
- Purpose: "describes the steps taken to and the trade-offs involved in constructing firm-level TFP measures using ORBIS"; shows "not all productivity measures can be calculated using readily available variables for all countries", proposes imputations and "assesses the accuracy of these imputations on a set of countries where ORBIS provides good coverage", finding imputed TFP "provide reasonable approximations for the 'true' values" — [RePEc abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/p/oec/ecoaaa/1049-en.html)
- Four TFP approaches: "the simplest Solow residual (least demanding in data), the superlative index (best suited for level comparisons), OLS-residuals (benchmark for production-function methods) and the Wooldridge (2009) method (most sophisticated but most demanding in terms of data)" — [USITC working paper summarising Gal 2013](https://www.usitc.gov/publications/332/working_papers/ahmad_oliver_peters_pdf.pdf)
- Imputation of labour costs: "total labour costs per employee are taken from OECD STAN by country, year, and 2-digit industry" and multiplied by the firm's number of employees in Orbis to obtain imputed firm-specific labour costs — [OECD PDF](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2013/05/measuring-total-factor-productivity-at-the-firm-level-using-oecd-orbis_g17a22d0/5k46dsb25ls6-en.pdf); [USITC summary](https://www.usitc.gov/publications/332/working_papers/ahmad_oliver_peters_pdf.pdf)
- Imputation of value added and intermediate inputs "using both available information in ORBIS and from industry-level data sources such as STAN" — [OECD PDF](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2013/05/measuring-total-factor-productivity-at-the-firm-level-using-oecd-orbis_g17a22d0/5k46dsb25ls6-en.pdf)
- Capital: firm investment and capital are built "by the perpetual inventory method so that capital in the current period is the sum of investment in the current period and the capital in the previous period less depreciation" — [OECD PDF](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2013/05/measuring-total-factor-productivity-at-the-firm-level-using-oecd-orbis_g17a22d0/5k46dsb25ls6-en.pdf)
- Cross-country comparability: "currency conversion based on PPPs and deflation", plus re-sampling/weighting to correct coverage — [USITC summary](https://www.usitc.gov/publications/332/working_papers/ahmad_oliver_peters_pdf.pdf)
- Downstream OECD papers describe the "Gal (2013)" cleaning convention as: keep accounts that refer to entire calendar years; drop observations with missing key variables and "outliers identified as implausible changes or ratios"; impute missing values as suggested by Gal (2013); exclude very small firms with fewer than 3 employees for data-quality reasons; exclude cases with negative value added — [OECD ECO/WKP(2021)1](https://one.oecd.org/document/ECO/WKP(2021)1/en/pdf); [OECD WP 1209, Labour Market Mismatch and Labour Productivity](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2015/04/labour-market-mismatch-and-labour-productivity_g17a2647/5js1pzx1r2kb-en.pdf) (attribution between these two OECD papers is from a merged snippet)
- Andrews, Criscuolo & Gal, "The Best versus the Rest" (OECD Productivity WP No. 5, 2016; CEP DP1645, 2019): Orbis "is a subsample of the universe of companies for most countries retaining larger and more productive firms"; "to mitigate problems from this under-representation of small firms, they restrict their sample to firms with more than 20 employees on average" — [CEP DP1645 (RePEc)](https://ideas.repec.org/p/cep/cepdps/dp1645.html); [OECD Productivity WP 5](https://ideas.repec.org/p/oec/ecoaac/5-en.html)
- Schwellnus, Pak, Pionnier & Crivellaro (2018), "Labour share developments over the past two decades", OECD ECO WP 1503 — uses Orbis firm-level data with industry investment prices; the firm-level methodology is in [ECO/WKP(2018)51](https://one.oecd.org/document/ECO/WKP(2018)51/En/pdf) and in the INSEE companion Pak, Pionnier & Schwellnus (2019), Economie et Statistique 510-511-512 — [article](https://www.insee.fr/en/statistiques/fichier/4253019/510_511_512_Pak_Pionnier_Schwellnus_EN.pdf); [online complements](https://insee.fr/en/statistiques/fichier/4253019/510-511-512_Pak_Pionnier_Schwellnus_complements_EN.pdf)

**Practical/format notes**
- Orbis web downloads come wide (one column per year per variable); Statalist thread on converting Orbis exports to long panel format using `egen panelid=group(...)` + `reshape long` — [Statalist 1753876](https://www.statalist.org/forums/forum/general-stata-discussion/general/1753876-restructuring-data-from-orbis)

### Inferences
- There is no single "consensus" consolidation rule: the OECD/IMF/Kalemli-Özcan strand prefers unconsolidated accounts (U1/U2, plus LF) to approximate national business-register units, while a minority (and apparently one sentence in the NBER revision) keeps consolidated accounts to avoid double counting group sales. The report should present both and recommend making the choice explicit and testable (e.g., compare aggregate turnover vs SBS under each rule).
- The Díez et al. appendix is the most explicit *published* list of numeric filters (negatives, >2m employees, liabilities ratio 0.9–1.1, non-positive age) and is directly transferable to Orbis Historical because it was built from the same account tables.
- The "closing date on/after 1 June → current year" rule implies a 12-month account ending 31 May is attributed to the previous year; combined with "keep accounts referring to entire calendar years" (OECD) it suggests also filtering NR_MONTHS = 12 (or annualising), but no snippet stated the NR_MONTHS rule explicitly (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- Exact treatment of accounts with number of months ≠ 12 (drop vs annualise) was not stated in any snippet for any of the papers.
- Units/currency: no snippet gave the explicit rule (e.g., use USD-converted tables `hindgfrusd` vs local currency with year-average vs closing-date exchange rates); Gal (2013) uses PPPs but the base year/source was not visible.
- Gal (2013) exact deflator source (STAN vs EU KLEMS), depreciation rates and initial capital for PIM, and the accuracy correlations of imputed vs true TFP were not retrievable.
- Ribeiro et al. (2010): the concrete "suspicious value" rules (ratio checks, balance-sheet identity tolerances) are in the PDF but not in any snippet.
- Kalemli-Özcan et al.: the exact winsorisation/trimming thresholds (one snippet mentions "winsorizing variables at the 99.9th percentile" in Orbis-based analysis but without clear attribution) remain unverified; the sentence "keeping the consolidated accounts" could not be attributed with confidence.
- Bajgar et al. (2020) country-by-country coverage percentages (France, Italy, Spain, Germany, US, Japan) are in the paper's tables but were not visible.

## Key Question 3 — Representativeness over time (1999–2008 vs later), by country and size class; comparison to Eurostat SBS / business registers

### Takeaway
All sources agree that Orbis coverage is good for large firms from the late 1990s but poor for SMEs before roughly 2005, improving thereafter; coverage of manufacturing gross output vs Eurostat SBS reaches ~60%+ in most European countries from 2001 after the Kalemli-Özcan cleaning, while the OECD's 2017-vintage benchmark shows ~60% of employment/output and ~40% of value added with a tilt toward larger, older, more productive firms even within size classes.

### Cited Findings
- Kalemli-Özcan et al. (2024): comparing manufacturing gross output in Orbis to Eurostat-SBS, "most countries show close to or above 60 percent coverage ratios, especially since 2001"; each country-year cell is the sum of manufacturing firms' gross output in Orbis over official manufacturing gross output in SBS; Spain and Italy are among the countries shown — [AEJ:Macro 2024 PDF](https://sebnemkalemliozcan.com/assets/publications/AEJMacro-2022-0036.pdf); [NBER WP 21558](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w21558/w21558.pdf)
- Bajgar et al. (2020): "while larger firms are generally well-covered from earlier years, smaller firms are not, with a coverage that improves over time"; Orbis "has very incomplete coverage of small and medium-sized firms in many countries, especially in the late 1990s and early 2000s" — [OECD STI WP 2020/06](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2020/05/coverage-and-representativeness-of-orbis-data_9628c322/c7bdaa03-en.pdf)
- Bajgar et al. (2020): ~60% of aggregate employment and output, ~40% of value added captured; tilt toward larger/older/more productive firms within each size class; reduced dispersion in the bottom half of the productivity distribution because underperforming firms are missing; Orbis "struggles to replicate patterns observed in official aggregate and firm-level data" for the full distribution and for entry/exit — [OECD STI WP 2020/06](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2020/05/coverage-and-representativeness-of-orbis-data_9628c322/c7bdaa03-en.pdf)
- Because MultiProd is representative of the population, "the set of top 10% of firms is measured consistently over time" there, whereas in Orbis the improving coverage confounds trends in the frontier — [OECD STI WP 2020/06](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2020/05/coverage-and-representativeness-of-orbis-data_9628c322/c7bdaa03-en.pdf)
- Andrews, Criscuolo & Gal restrict to firms with >20 employees on average precisely because Orbis under-represents small firms — [CEP DP1645](https://ideas.repec.org/p/cep/cepdps/dp1645.html)
- Earlier OECD assessment: Ribeiro et al. (2010) identify "structural biases in the database and the steps that should be taken to correct them" — [OECD Statistics WP 2010/01](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2010/03/the-oecd-orbis-database_g17a1e2e/5kmhds8mzj8w-en.pdf)
- USITC (Ahmad, Oliver & Peters) review of cross-country firm-level productivity comparisons discusses Orbis coverage versus SBS and the Gal (2013) re-weighting/PPP adjustments — [USITC working paper](https://www.usitc.gov/publications/332/working_papers/ahmad_oliver_peters_pdf.pdf)
- Italy-specific: De Socio & Finaldi Russo (2016), "The debt of Italian non-financial firms: an international comparison", Banca d'Italia Questioni di Economia e Finanza (Occasional Papers) No. 308, February 2016 — uses Orbis for euro-area firms (data 2004–2013, 18 euro-area countries, ~1 million NFCs) and Cerved for Italy, noting Cerved "includes virtually all Italian limited liability companies" and that in Cerved "around 40 per cent of firms without financial debt report a positive amount of total debt but not the specific values for financial debt or other kind of debt" — [QEF 308 PDF](https://www.bancaditalia.it/pubblicazioni/qef/2016-0308/QEF_308_16.pdf)
- Italy-specific (BvD national product): AIDA (BvD) holds "financial statements, registry and sector data from the last 10 years for the top 900,000 Italian companies, both active and bankrupt, from 2003 to today, excluding banks, insurance companies, and public entities"; one study found the AIDA–Cerved correspondence "very high: only 32 patenting firms stored in AIDA (more than 8,500) are not in Cerved" — [Univ. Brescia AIDA page](https://www.unibs.it/en/aida-computerised-analysis-italian-companies); [Banca d'Italia QEF 313 (2016)](https://www.bancaditalia.it/pubblicazioni/qef/2016-0313/QEF_313_16.pdf) (attribution of the 32/8,500 figure to QEF 313 is inferred from the result set, unverified)
- CompNet's Italian data come from Chamber of Commerce financial statements (i.e., the Cerved/InfoCamere universe) and Istat rather than Orbis, which is why CompNet can be used as a near-population benchmark for Italy — [CompNet 8th Vintage User Guide](https://www.comp-net.org/fileadmin/_compnet/user_upload/2023-10-31%208th%20Vintage%20User%20Guide%20Final.pdf)

### Inferences
- For Italy, Orbis Historical's underlying source is the same Chamber-of-Commerce filings that feed Cerved/AIDA, so coverage of limited-liability companies should be close to universal from the mid-2000s; the main Italian gaps are partnerships/sole proprietorships (not required to file) and employment (not in the financial statements, hence often missing). This is an inference consistent with the SBS-coverage ratios above, not a verified statement from a source.
- Representativeness tests should be run per country-year-size-class against SBS (number of firms, employment, turnover/gross output, value added), exactly as Kalemli-Özcan et al. and Bajgar et al. do; before ~2005 any SME-level conclusion for most countries is fragile.

### Gaps
- No Banca d'Italia Temi di Discussione was found that directly benchmarks Orbis/AIDA against Cerved for productivity analysis; only the QEF 308 debt comparison surfaced. A targeted search of bancaditalia.it (Temi/QEF with "Orbis") is needed.
- Country-by-country, year-by-year coverage tables (Bajgar et al. 2020; Kalemli-Özcan et al. 2024 Figures/Tables) could not be read, so no numbers for France, Germany, US, Japan are reported here.
- Nothing was found on how Orbis Historical coverage for 1995–1999 compares with Amadeus vintages of that era.

## Key Question 4 — Publicly released Stata/Python replication code

### Takeaway
The only fully public, end-to-end Orbis construction code found is the Kalemli-Özcan team's suite (UMD orbis.html programs + openICPSR replication package for the AEJ:Macro 2024 paper); OECD, IMF and CompNet publish methodology documents but not raw-Orbis cleaning code (CompNet's code runs inside national statistical institutes on non-Orbis data).

### Cited Findings
- openICPSR project 187541 (V1): replication package for AEJ:Macro 2022-0036 (Kalemli-Özcan et al. 2024), containing a README and the folder "AEJMacro-2022-0036_Replication_June2023" — [openICPSR 187541](https://openicpsr.org/openicpsr/project/187541/version/V1/view); AEA article page with appendix link — [aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/mac.20220036.appx](https://aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/mac.20220036.appx)
- "Detailed guides and programs to process this historical data are available at http://econweb.umd.edu/~kalemli/orbis.html" — [NBER WP 21558 rev.0](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w21558/revisions/w21558.rev0.pdf); processing guide PDF — [Processing Orbis Historical Disk](https://sebnemkalemliozcan.com/assets/workingpapers/Processing%20Orbis%20Historical%20Disk%20FINAL2.pdf); NBER ORBIS project page — [nber.org/research/orbis](https://www.nber.org/research/orbis)
- CompNet: the outlier/cleaning code is "centrally implemented in the set of codes" distributed to national data providers (not applied to Orbis) — [CompNet user guide round 4](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/home/pdf/research/compnet/CompNet-database-user_guide-round4.pdf)
- General-purpose (not Orbis-specific) data-processing guidance with Stata/R/Python examples: Ouellet & Toffel (2024), HBS WP 25-010 — [HBS PDF](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/25-010_1b83b5ca-9263-47a9-81af-a02855e49842.pdf); [SSRN 4951066](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4951066)
- A World Bank reproducibility package exists for an Orbis-based study (catalogue entry 464) — [reproducibility.worldbank.org catalog/464](https://reproducibility.worldbank.org//catalog/464/download/1325) (content not inspected)

### Inferences
- The openICPSR package is likely restricted to code (Orbis is proprietary), so the do-files should be usable as a template for Orbis Historical flat files after adapting table/variable names.

### Gaps
- No GitHub repository dedicated to Orbis/Orbis Historical cleaning was found via search; the search returned only generic Stata cleaning repos. Replication code for Díez–Fan–Villegas-Sánchez (JIE 2021), Gopinath et al. (QJE 2017) and De Loecker–Obermeier–Van Reenen (2022) was not located (the QJE Dataverse and JIE supplementary materials should be checked directly).
- The contents of the openICPSR README (do-file names, required raw tables, software versions) could not be read.

## Consolidated quick-reference of exact rules found (with source)

| Step | Rule as stated in source | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Account type | Keep U1, U2 and LF (unknown); drop C1/C2 unless firm continuously reports consolidated | Díez–Fan–Villegas-Sánchez IMF WP 19/82 |
| Duplicate BvD ID–year | Priority to unconsolidated for continuously-unconsolidated reporters; priority to consolidated for continuously-consolidated reporters | Díez–Fan–Villegas-Sánchez IMF WP 19/82 |
| Duplicate from changed closing month | Keep the closing date "closest conceptually to the end of year" | Kalemli-Özcan et al. Data Description 2015 |
| Duplicate from filing type (Historical Orbis UK) | Remove duplicates; "use observations at the highest level of aggregation" (Annual Report vs Local Registry Filing) | De Loecker–Obermeier–Van Reenen 2022 appendix |
| Fiscal year | Closing date on/after 1 June → current calendar year (before → previous year) | Kalemli-Özcan et al. Data Description 2015 |
| Calendar-year accounts | Keep accounts that refer to entire calendar years | OECD papers following Gal (2013) |
| Whole-firm drops | Negative total assets, employment, sales or tangible fixed assets in any year; employment > 2,000,000 ("Walmart") in any year | Díez et al.; Gourinchas et al. (citing Kalemli-Özcan et al.) |
| Firm-year drops | Non-positive age; non-positive liabilities; two liability measures with ratio >1.1 or <0.9; negative intangibles; zero/negative fixed assets; missing both costs of employees and employees; negative current/non-current liabilities, current assets, loans | Díez et al. IMF WP 19/82 |
| TFP inputs (gross-output PF) | Drop missing/zero/negative gross output, wage bill, capital stock, materials | Gopinath et al. QJE 2017 |
| Deflators | Eurostat 2-digit gross-output deflators (output, wage bill); investment-goods price for capital | Gopinath et al. QJE 2017 |
| Deflators (CompNet) | NACE Rev.2 2-digit sector deflators; GDP deflator for turnover/capital/intermediates (early rounds); VA PPPs for cross-country | CompNet round-4 guide |
| Capital | PIM: K_t = I_t + (1−δ)K_{t−1} | Gal 2013 |
| Labour cost imputation | STAN labour cost per employee (country × year × 2-digit) × Orbis employees | Gal 2013 |
| Size floor | >20 employees on average (Andrews–Criscuolo–Gal); ≥20 (CompNet 20E); ≥10 (Bajgar benchmark; De Loecker et al.); <3 employees dropped (OECD papers) | respective sources |
| Outliers | Negative VA → missing; growth rates trimmed at 1st/99th pct (CompNet early); ratios to capital/labour in top/bottom 1% within 2-digit sector-year → missing (CompNet 8th); markups/TFP winsorised 1st/99th by country or within 2-digit industry (markup literature) | CompNet guides; Hitchhiker's Guide to Markup Estimation |
