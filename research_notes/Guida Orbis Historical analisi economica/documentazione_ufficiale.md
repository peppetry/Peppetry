## Key Question 1 — What does Moody's/BvD officially say Orbis Historical contains (time span, start year, countries, deleted/inactive companies, ownership history)?

### Takeaway
Across every institutional description that reproduces the vendor's text, Orbis Historical is defined as a point-in-time snapshot/archive of the Orbis database containing **all company financials BvD has collected back to 1995** (some guides say "from the 1990s" or "around 2000") and **ownership (shareholder/subsidiary link) history from 2007 onwards**, delivered outside the web interface as bulk tab-delimited text (or SQL/parquet via Moody's DataHub), refreshed twice a year; the latest guide (Michigan Kresge) states financials 1995 to June 2025 and ownership 2007 to June 2025. The product is global (same country universe as Orbis); none of the accessible sources gives an official country list specific to Orbis Historical.

### Inferences
- The two "update" calendars reported (April/October in HBS/Stanford/CRL text; June/December in Kresge 2025 and Illinois "December and June" folders; June 2021/2024/2025 end-dates in Yale/CRL/Kresge) are consistent with the delivery cadence having moved to June/December vintages; a 2025 delivery is most likely labelled by its cut-off month (e.g., "June 2025").
- "Point-in-time snapshot" in vendor text refers to the *descriptive* (header) tables being a snapshot at the delivery date, while financial and ownership tables are cumulative histories (see the CRL/Princeton wording in Key Question 5). Replication code confirms: one Legal_info/Contact/Industry file (current state) plus long-format financial history and one Links file per year.
- No source says Orbis Historical purges companies; the fact that it is the vendor's own answer to the 10-year window and to firm drop-outs (UWE, Kresge, Kalemli-Özcan) implies inactive/dissolved firms and their old statements remain in the archive. This is inference, not an official guarantee.

### Gaps
- No official country list or firm counts specific to Orbis Historical were found (hosts blocked).
- No official statement that Orbis Historical "retains deleted companies" verbatim; only indirect evidence.
- The Moody's DataHub "ORBIS Data Dictionary" (mentioned by CBS) could not be opened.

## Key Question 2 — Exact file/table names in a bulk Orbis Historical delivery and their columns

### Takeaway
Replication code from four independent licence-holders (2020-06, 2022-06, 2023-01 DataHub, 2025-12 deliveries) shows a stable structure: a **Financials** folder with `Industry-Global_financials_and_ratios-{USD|EUR}.txt` and `Key_financials-{USD|EUR}.txt` (long format, one row per statement), a **Descriptive** folder with `Legal_info.txt`, `Industry_classifications.txt`, `Contact_info`, `Trade_description.txt`, `Overviews.txt`, `Bankers-current.txt`, `Other_advisors-current.txt` (one row per BvD ID, current snapshot), and an **Ownership** folder with `Entities.txt`, `Links_2007.txt` … `Links_2020.txt` and `Links_current.txt`. Files are tab-delimited, ISO-8859-1/UTF-8 with a BOM on the first header, shipped as multi-part `.rar` archives with `.md5` checksums; the BvD ID number is the join key everywhere.

## Key Question 3 — Record key of a financial statement; values of "Consolidation code" and "Filing type"

### Takeaway
A statement row is identified by **BvD ID number × Consolidation code × Filing type × Closing date** (with `Number of months`, `Accounting practice`, `Source`, `Original units`, `Original currency`, `Exchange rate` as attributes); multiple rows per firm-year are normal. Consolidation codes are **C1, C2, U1, U2, LF** with BvD's standard definitions (C1/U1 = no companion statement; C2/U2 = companion statement exists; LF = limited financials); **NF/NRF appear in Orbis as company-level "no (recent) financials" flags, not as statement rows, and were not confirmed in any source accessible here**. Filing types observed are **"Annual report"** and **"Local registry filing"**.

### Cited Findings
- Primary key declared in a DataHub pipeline for `industry_global_financials_and_ratios`: `("bvd_id_number", "consolidation_code", "filing_type", "closing_date")`, doc string "Firm-year financial statements. Multiple filings per firm-year are normal." (GitHub replication code) — [usama-jamal/firmpanel schemas.py](https://github.com/usama-jamal/firmpanel)
- WRDS guidance: "Start with one `bvdid` and one fiscal year. Identify multiple consolidations, filing types, closing dates, and annual versus interim periods before choosing the panel key. Do not collapse them by arbitrary maximum values." `nr_months` is `varchar(2)`: "period length is not safely assumed numeric/12". (GitHub, WRDS metadata) — [piotrek-orlowski/claude-wrds-public bvd.md](https://github.com/piotrek-orlowski/claude-wrds-public)
- Consolidation code definitions (BvD wording reproduced in LexisNexis's Orbis report help): "C1: Statement of a mother company integrating the statements of its controlled subsidiaries or branches with no unconsolidated companion. C2: Statement of a mother company integrating the statements of its controlled subsidiaries or branches with an unconsolidated companion. U1: Statement not integrating the statements of the possible controlled subsidiaries or branches of the concerned company with no unconsolidated companion. U2: Statement not integrating the statements of the possible controlled subsidiaries or branches of the concerned company with a unconsolidated companion. LF (Limited Financials): There are several countries where detailed financials are not available for some companies. In such case, LF are quite often made of median value of turnover range (or turnover estimate), median value of employee range (or estimate) and current capital." (snippet) — [LexisNexis Risk, "Orbis Report (Global Company Coverage) Results"](https://riskmanagement.lexisnexis.com/bps/web20_help/RSKM/bsp_results_orbisreport_r.html); same list at [MatchPlat, "Consolidation codes"](https://www.matchplat.com/en/consolidation-codes) and [VU Amsterdam libguide (Zephyr/BvD)](https://libguides.vu.nl/finding-data/zephyr)
- Note: the U2 definition as quoted says "with a unconsolidated companion"; BvD's intended meaning (and all third-party usage) is "with a *consolidated* companion", i.e. U2 = unconsolidated statement of a firm that also files consolidated accounts. (Interpretation; see Inferences.)
- Academic usage (IFS Deaton Review appendix, snippet): "The LF consolidation code indicates the firm reports limited financial information. More specifically, Orbis contains companies with accounts of type LF with limited financial information (number of employees, turnover, shareholders' funds (book equity), capital, or some of these items)." — [IFS Deaton Review, Firms online appendices](https://www.nuffieldfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Firms-and-inequalities-online-appendices-IFS-Deaton-Review.pdf)
- Observed code sets in raw data: "Before doing this, I have U1, U2, C1, and C2 and LF" (GitHub replication code) — [gabpiazza/Big-Bang, 01_dataprep_B.R](https://github.com/gabpiazza/Big-Bang); `UNCONSOLIDATED = ("U1","U2")`, `CONSOLIDATED = ("C1","C2","LF")` — [usama-jamal/firmpanel](https://github.com/usama-jamal/firmpanel) (the LF-as-consolidated grouping is this author's choice, not a vendor rule); WRDS users rank `C1 > C2 > U1 > U2` and `Annual report > other` when deduplicating — [Leslie-creat/rct25, create_orbis_panel_de.R](https://github.com/Leslie-creat/rct25)
- Filing type values: "Some firms have both an observation stemming from the 'Annual Report' and one from 'Local Registry Filing', with the Annual Report typically containing more information on key variables like revenue, COGS, wage bill and employment"; researchers "remove Local Registry Filing whenever there is also an Annual Report available." (snippet) — [IFS Deaton Review appendices](https://www.nuffieldfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Firms-and-inequalities-online-appendices-IFS-Deaton-Review.pdf); also "we first keep data with the filing type of 'annual report' instead of 'local registry filing'. If we still observe multiple sources ... we only keep data with the consolidation type of 'C2'" — [ahmadlp, Markups as shadow tariffs (paper appendix)](https://github.com/ahmadlp/ahmadlp.github.io); exact string values `"Annual report"` and `"Local registry filing"` used in synthetic Orbis-shaped data and in R filters `filing_type == "Annual report"` — [usama-jamal/firmpanel synth.py](https://github.com/usama-jamal/firmpanel), [Leslie-creat/rct25](https://github.com/Leslie-creat/rct25), [gabpiazza/Big-Bang 01_dataprep_C.R](https://github.com/gabpiazza/Big-Bang)
- Related Orbis company-level fields (Orbis API field list, as reproduced by a software vendor): `CONSOLIDATION_CODE=Consolidation code`, `FILING_TYPE=Filing type`, `CLOSING_DATE_LAST_ANNUAL_ACCOUNTS`, `CLOSING_DATE_LAST_INTERIM_ACCOUNTS`, `ACCOUNTING_TEMPLATE=Accounting template`, `FILING_EXEMPTION=Reason of filing exemption`, `NUMBER_MONTHS=Number of months`, `AUDIT_STATUS=Audit status`, `ACCOUNTS_STATUS=Accounts status`, `ACCOUNTING_PRACTICE=Accounting practice`, `ACCOUNTS_SOURCE_CODE=Source of financials`, `ORIGINAL_CURRENCY`, `EXCHANGE_RATE=Exchange rate from original currency`. (GitHub, CluedIn documentation) — [CluedIn-io/CluedIn.Documentation, enricher/220-bvd.md](https://github.com/CluedIn-io/CluedIn.Documentation/blob/master/docs/050-preparation/enricher/220-bvd.md)

### Inferences
- Because U1/U2 and C1/C2 are defined relative to the *existence of a companion statement*, the code can differ across years for the same firm; firms in U2/C2 years contribute two rows for the same closing date, which is why the key must include `Consolidation code` and `Filing type`.
- "NF"/"NRF" (No financials / No recent financials) are Orbis *company-status* labels seen in web-interface search results (company has no, or no recent, accounts); since they describe absence of statements they do not generate rows in the financial tables and would not appear in `Consolidation code`. This matches the fact that no replication script handling raw Orbis Historical files mentions NF/NRF. This is inference; no accessible source documented NF/NRF.
- `Source (for publicly quoted companies)` identifies the data provider for listed companies' accounts (e.g., a data vendor vs. filing); the long WRDS comment implies it is populated mainly for quoted firms.

### Gaps
- No official Moody's/BvD page defining NF/NRF or listing the full domain of `Filing type`, `Accounting practice`, `Audit status`, `Source` could be accessed.
- Whether 2025 deliveries have introduced additional filing types (e.g., for estimates or interim accounts flagged separately) is unknown.

## Key Question 4 — Missing-value coding, units and currency fields

### Takeaway
In raw tab-delimited Orbis Historical files, absent values are empty strings; researchers also guard against the Orbis conventions `n.a.` (not available) and `n.s.` (not significant), which appear in Orbis web exports and ratio fields. Monetary values in the `-EUR`/`-USD` files are in **absolute units (not thousands)** after conversion at `Exchange rate from original currency`; `Original units` and `Original currency` record the scale and currency of the source filing; per-employee ratios are labelled "(th)" (thousands).

### Cited Findings
- "Operating-revenue values in this file [`Key_financials-EUR.txt`] are absolute EUR (verified: Walmart US710415188 ~ EUR 563bn), NOT thousands." Numeric parsing treats a field as missing when empty or starting with a letter (`if not x or x[0].isalpha(): return None`). (GitHub replication code, June-2022 vintage) — [Tax-Justice-Network/unitary-taxation 1b_1_financials.py](https://github.com/Tax-Justice-Network/unitary-taxation)
- Orbis web export of the same item is in thousands: "Operating revenue (Turnover) th USD 2016..2025" and "Orbis revenue is in thousands USD"; the same project's parser nulls the tokens `""`, `"n.a."`, `"n.s."`, `"na"`, `"N/A"`, `"-"`. (GitHub replication code) — [Tax-Justice-Network/unitary-taxation 3_32_build_orbis_entity_universe.py](https://github.com/Tax-Justice-Network/unitary-taxation)
- An Orbis Europe Excel-export parser describes values that "may contain commas, comparison operators, or N/A markers" and maps `"n.a."`, `"n/a"`, `"N/A"`, `"n.s."` to NaN (GitHub replication code) — [ROHITH-RAMAKRISHNAPPA/Irish-SME-Dissolution-Risk, src/extract_orbis.py](https://github.com/ROHITH-RAMAKRISHNAPPA/Irish-SME-Dissolution-Risk)
- Definition of the two tokens as used in BvD-style ratio reporting (third-party documentation, snippet): "N.A. (not available) indicates that an input the ratio needs is missing from the filing ... N.S. (not significant) means the ratio is computable but the result would mislead ... The cases that produce N.S. are a zero denominator, a return measured against negative shareholders' funds, and a value so extreme it overflows." — [Kausate docs, Financial Ratios](https://docs.kausate.com/guides/financial-ratios) (not a Moody's document)
- Dec-2025 text files: the extraction treats "Total assets non vuoto" (empty string) as missing; `Number of months` is compared as the string `"12"`; closing date is `YYYYMMDD` text (`substr($4,1,4)` = year). (GitHub replication code) — [agostinognasso/panel-e2tree 00_extract_orbis.sh](https://github.com/agostinognasso/panel-e2tree)
- Units/currency metadata columns present in the historical files: `Original units`, `Original currency`, `Exchange rate from original currency` (text headers) = WRDS `orig_units` varchar(25), `orig_currency` varchar(3), `exchrate` double. (GitHub) — [YiLulululu bvdColumns.py](https://github.com/YiLulululu/EquityOwnershipStructure); [rs-kellogg WRDS notebook](https://github.com/rs-kellogg/wrds_workshop_public)
- WRDS guidance: "Retain the full ... fields `number_months`, `fiscal_year`, ... `original_unit`, `original_currency`, `usd_exchange_rate`, and `eur_exchange_rate` when validating periods, scale, and currency basis" (Bank Focus) and for Orbis "`orig_units`, `orig_currency` — retain when comparing converted values"; "Product currency labels do not by themselves establish measurement scale." — [piotrek-orlowski/claude-wrds-public bvd.md](https://github.com/piotrek-orlowski/claude-wrds-public)
- Ratio headers carry their unit in the name: "(%)", "(x)", "(days)", "(th)" for per-employee amounts; market capitalisation is "MARKET CAPITALISATION (MIL)". — [YiLulululu bvdColumns.py](https://github.com/YiLulululu/EquityOwnershipStructure); [rs-kellogg notebook](https://github.com/rs-kellogg/wrds_workshop_public)
- A number-of-employees column coexists with `Estimated employees`, `Estimated operating revenue`, `Operating revenue original range value`, `Employees  original range value` (range/estimate provenance for LF-type records). — [YiLulululu bvdColumns.py](https://github.com/YiLulululu/EquityOwnershipStructure)
- Ownership percentages are strings: WRDS `_9009`/`_9010` are `varchar(7)`; "Ownership percentages are strings: preserve raw values and separate parseable numeric values from special tokens, missing values, and bounds"; Orbis Historical `Links` files have both `Direct %` and `Direct % (only figures)`. — [piotrek-orlowski bvd.md](https://github.com/piotrek-orlowski/claude-wrds-public); [YiLulululu processHist.py](https://github.com/YiLulululu/EquityOwnershipStructure)

### Inferences
- The absolute-units finding (EUR file) plus the presence of `Original units` implies the bulk files already apply the unit multiplier and the exchange rate; `Original units` is informational (e.g., "thousands"/"units"/"millions" in the source filing) rather than a multiplier still to be applied. The exact domain of `Original units` was not found in any accessible source.
- Zero is a legitimate value and is not used as a missing-value code in the bulk files (code tests `> 0` only for economic filtering).
- Ownership `Direct %` tokens such as `WO` (wholly owned), `MO` (majority owned), `NG` (negligible), `>50`, `<0.01` are standard Orbis conventions and explain the "(only figures)" variant, but no accessible source listed them for Orbis Historical specifically.

### Gaps
- Official domain lists for `Original units`, `Audit status`, `Accounting practice`, `Source`, and ownership percentage tokens were not retrievable.
- Whether Orbis Historical text files ever contain literal `n.a.`/`n.s.` strings (as opposed to blanks) is not confirmed; the TJN parser's "starts with a letter" guard suggests alphabetic tokens can occur.

## Key Question 5 — Retention rules of standard Orbis vs Orbis Historical; "as published" vs revised data

### Takeaway
Officially (as relayed by libraries), standard Orbis keeps a rolling **10-year financial window** (some CBS text: 10 years for listed, 5 for others) and drops companies after roughly 10 years of inactivity; Amadeus historically deleted firms inactive 4-6 years (survivorship bias); Orbis Historical exists precisely to keep the pre-window financials (from 1995) and ownership snapshots (from 2007). Vendor-derived text states that in each delivery "descriptive data is not cumulative or historical" while financials/ownership are "cumulatively historical". **No source states that Orbis Historical stores statements "as published" without later revisions**; researchers document that values for a given fiscal year differ across vintages and that reporting lags of ~2 years mean later vintages add (not just revise) observations.

### Cited Findings
- "Subscription to Orbis includes access to 10 years of historical data, with more historical data available through a subscription to Orbis Historical." (snippet) — [UWE Bristol](https://www.uwe.ac.uk/study/library/databases/a-z/orbis)
- "Each delivery of data includes all the years; however, descriptive data is not cumulative or historical—it is a snapshot of each period and therefore is header data that cannot be used for historical industry or geography." "Global format histories are cumulative files with data back to 1997 with the exception of finance and industry which is current only." "Companies are dropped after roughly 10 years if not active." "ORBIS Historical provides point in time snapshots of ORBIS back to 2020, with ownership files going back to 2007." (snippets returned together; originating page is one of [Princeton Library FAQ 11030](https://faq.library.princeton.edu/econ/faq/11030), [Princeton FAQ 11082](https://faq.library.princeton.edu/econ/faq/11082), [CRL eDesiderata node 549](https://edesiderata.crl.edu/node/549) — hosts blocked, so attribution could not be pinned)
- "A key difference is historical coverage. Fame has 20 years of financial history whereas Orbis has 10." "Companies in Amadeus were deleted if inactive for 4-6 years, creating survivorship bias; this is not the case with Fame and Orbis." (snippet) — [University of Manchester Library FAQ 259496](https://manchester-uk.libanswers.com/teaching-and-learning/faq/259496)
- "Amadeus drops the firm if firm did not report anything in the last 6 years where Orbis keeps the firm and if firm exits, turn status to inactive." (snippet of Kalemli-Özcan et al.) — [NBER WP 21558 / AEJ:Macro appendix](https://aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/mac.20220036.appx); working paper [NBER w21558](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21558.pdf); data description [UMD Data_Description_2015_09_final.pdf](https://www.econ.umd.edu/sites/www.econ.umd.edu/files/pubs/Data_Description_2015_09_final.pdf)
- "The system operates a 10-year window for listed companies and a 5-year window for all other companies." (snippet, CBS Orbis description) — [CBS Library Orbis pages](https://libguides.cbs.dk/orbis)
- Web-platform window as experienced by users: "the ORBIS' online portal updates company data when new data becomes available, however, it only provides 10 years of financial information for a company ... when the latest financial data of a company becomes available in 2016, the ORBIS' online portal will drop all data of this company before 2007" (data downloaded May 2024). (GitHub paper source) — [ahmadlp, Markups as shadow tariffs](https://github.com/ahmadlp/ahmadlp.github.io)
- Vintage effects documented by Kalemli-Özcan et al. (snippet): "There is a reporting lag of about 2 years on average, and there are differences in the coverage of particular variables depending on when the BvD product has been released. For example, for the 2010 vintage, a company may not have the 2010 filings but the 2010 filings will appear in the 2012 vintage. BvD's data collection improves over time and this lag might vary by country and by data vintage." "Good coverage relative to Census can be achieved if one merges across different vintages of data or uses the Orbis historical database." — [AEJ:Macro 2024 appendix](https://aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/mac.20220036.appx); [Tinbergen DP 15-110](https://papers.tinbergen.nl/15110.pdf)
- Historical-disk processing by the same team is documented in a stand-alone PDF "PROCESSING ORBIS HISTORICAL DISK" (stages 1-4; Stage 4 "provides .dta files separated by country, with long string variables dropped and interim/Eurodollar files removed. New files merge industry class information to the financial files, and ownership files are supplemented with entity data.") (snippet) — [sebnemkalemliozcan.com PDF](https://sebnemkalemliozcan.com/assets/workingpapers/Processing%20Orbis%20Historical%20Disk%20FINAL2.pdf); mirror [fanjt.weebly.com orbisdocumentation.pdf](https://fanjt.weebly.com/uploads/1/9/4/7/19473457/orbisdocumentation.pdf)
- Orbis ownership archive on the web platform: "Orbis keeps archived data in its Ownership database since January 2003. You can access information about Global Ultimate Owner (GUO), shareholders, direct- and total ownership..." (snippet) — [CBS Company Information libguide, Ownership](https://libguides.cbs.dk/Company_Information/Ownership)
- Standard-Orbis bulk export used in 2025 ("snapshot Dicembre 2025") contained statements "dal 2000 al 2024 circa", i.e., more than 10 years in a bulk (non-web) export. (GitHub replication code) — [agostinognasso/panel-e2tree DATA_REPORT.md](https://github.com/agostinognasso/panel-e2tree)
- `Legal_info` in bulk deliveries carries a `Historical record flag`, and a separate `statushistory` table records every status change with `Status date` and `Status updated in Orbis` (TRR 266 used it to date when a firm lost "Active" status). (GitHub replication code) — [trr266/disclosure_regulation_around_the_world](https://github.com/trr266/disclosure_regulation_around_the_world)

### Inferences
- "Point-in-time snapshots of ORBIS back to 2020" (Princeton/CRL wording) most likely means that the library's own archive of full Orbis Historical deliveries starts in 2020, not that the product's content starts in 2020.
- Because Orbis Historical is itself re-delivered twice a year as a *cumulative* history, each delivery reflects BvD's current (possibly corrected/restated) version of old statements; nothing indicates that superseded versions are preserved. A user wanting true "as published" vintages needs multiple deliveries (which is exactly what Kalemli-Özcan's team did with yearly disks).
- The `Historical record flag` in `Legal_info` suggests the descriptive table in a Historical delivery can include rows for entities no longer in current Orbis (flagged as historical records); this would be the mechanism by which deleted companies are retained. Unverified.

### Gaps
- No official Moody's statement on revisions/restatements policy in Orbis Historical was found.
- The exact current rule for removing companies from standard Orbis (10 years inactive? status-based?) is only available as library paraphrase.

## Key Question 7 — "BvD ID changes" correspondence tables and ID handling in Orbis Historical

### Takeaway
BvD IDs do change (country prefix + national identifier; re-keying when identifiers change), and Moody's exposes a "BvD ID change lookup" in its integrations, but no publicly documented correspondence table for Orbis Historical was found; the historical ownership files handle entities via `Entities.txt` (BvD ID of subsidiary or shareholder) and the NBER description says BvD "links several vintages/disks ... through firm identifiers". Researchers note the two-letter prefix encodes the country and that IDs are not uniformly formatted.

### Cited Findings
- "BvD ID changes in Orbis are tracked through a BvD ID Change Lookup Process in systems like Salesforce, which allows users to identify changed or removed BvD IDs and relink records to updated IDs." (snippet of a Moody's Salesforce AppExchange document) — [AppExchange PDF 14fa5c88-3f7d-4622-9aef-74af3e713c06](https://appexchange.salesforce.com/image_host/14fa5c88-3f7d-4622-9aef-74af3e713c06.pdf); context: [Jinfo, "What's changed on BvD's Orbis?"](https://jinfo.com/go/sub/article/72076); [INSEAD Library, Company identifiers](https://www.insead.edu/library/company-identifiers); [CDQ BVD data source](https://meta.cdq.com/Data_source/BVD)
- "BvD has developed a 'Historical Product' which links several vintages/disks of the Orbis data through firm identifiers" (snippet) — [NBER ORBIS project](https://www.nber.org/research/orbis)
- Illinois DSRS: "The BvD ID Number serves as a primary key or foreign key across most tables within this dataset. Detailed information can be retrieved from the Orbis database using the BvD ID" (snippet) — [DSRS BvD Historicals overview](https://dsrs.illinois.edu/internal/datahub/category/bvd-historicals/data-overview)
- ID format facts from code: "The BvD ID number encodes the country in its first two characters, so `substr($1,1,2)` selects the country"; "Vendor identifier formats ... are not uniform — `CHCHE101049653`, `PA843753RPP`, `GBSC177553`, `AR30-52531837-7`. Anything assuming `^[A-Z]{2}\d+$` breaks on real data." Links rows whose IDs contain `-` were dropped by one author (IDs with hyphens exist). (GitHub replication code) — [agostinognasso/panel-e2tree](https://github.com/agostinognasso/panel-e2tree); [usama-jamal/firmpanel README](https://github.com/usama-jamal/firmpanel); [YiLulululu processHist.py](https://github.com/YiLulululu/EquityOwnershipStructure)
- WRDS identity table `bvd.ob_w_company_id_table_l` holds `bvdid`, `lei_lei`, `sd_ticker`, `sd_isin`, "historical-name/status dates"; WRDS linking describes BvD security linking through ISIN/CUSIP/SEDOL only; "it does not supply one-to-one company mappings or validate their historical intervals." — [piotrek-orlowski bvd.md](https://github.com/piotrek-orlowski/claude-wrds-public)
- Orbis API exposes `BVD_ID_NUMBER`, `BVD_ACCOUNT_NUMBER=BvD account number`, `ORBISID=Orbis ID number`, `NATIONAL_ID`, `LEI`, and status fields `STATUS`, `STATUS_DATE`, `STATUS_CHANGE_DATE`, `LOCAL_STATUS` etc., i.e., several identifier layers per entity. (GitHub, CluedIn documentation) — [CluedIn-io/CluedIn.Documentation 220-bvd.md](https://github.com/CluedIn-io/CluedIn.Documentation/blob/master/docs/050-preparation/enricher/220-bvd.md)

### Inferences
- A "BvD ID changes" table (old ID → new ID) is a standard component of Moody's bulk/feed products (it is what the Salesforce lookup consumes); a 2025 Orbis Historical delivery may include such a file, but none of the five replication packages examined references one, so its presence in the Historical package is unconfirmed.
- The `BVD_ACCOUNT_NUMBER` / `ORBISID` layers suggest Moody's maintains an internal stable key beneath the BvD ID; researchers only see the BvD ID.

### Gaps
- No official file name/layout for a BvD-ID correspondence table in Orbis Historical was found.
- No documentation on how Orbis Historical treats mergers of duplicate records or re-issued IDs across vintages.
